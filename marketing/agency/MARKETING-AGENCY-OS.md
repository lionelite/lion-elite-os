# Lion Elite Marketing Agency Operating System

Updated: July 24, 2026

## Mission
Build a repeatable client-acquisition and Meta Ads service that can be sold to businesses. We do not reinvent proven direct-response principles. We study what successful advertisers repeatedly do, adapt it to the client's economics and market, execute, measure, and document.

## Research standard
For major strategic decisions, consult at least 10 independent publishers/operators when practical. Multiple pages from one publisher count as one source. Weight evidence by: actual advertiser results/case studies; large cross-account datasets; experienced operator evidence; official platform mechanics; then generic commentary. Anecdotal tactics that repeatedly work across credible operators can be used as starting hypotheses without waiting to rediscover them from zero. Client/account data becomes the final authority once sufficient clean data exists.

## Core business promise
We sell a measurable customer-acquisition system, not boosted posts. Scope can include offer positioning, Meta campaign strategy/build, creative strategy, ad copy, landing-page recommendations, Pixel/CAPI/tracking QA, lead routing, optimization, reporting, and growth experiments. Never guarantee revenue or ROAS.

## Ideal initial clients
Start where economics are understandable and outcomes can be tracked: local/service businesses, coaching/fitness, beauty/personal care, home services, professional services, ecommerce and other legitimate businesses with adequate margins and fulfillment capacity. Screen regulated/restricted categories separately for platform and legal compliance.

## Client qualification gate
Before accepting a client, establish: offer; price/AOV; gross margin; geographic market; ideal customer; monthly revenue; fulfillment capacity; current lead/customer volume; historical ad spend/results; website/landing page; CRM/sales process; close rate; LTV/repeat purchases; tracking status; creative assets; proof/testimonials with permission; legal/platform restrictions; monthly ad budget; decision maker; response-time capability.

Reject or repair-first when: no clear offer, economics cannot support acquisition, fulfillment is broken, claims are deceptive/unsubstantiated, client expects guaranteed results, tracking access is refused, or sales follow-up cannot handle leads.

## Economics before ads
Calculate allowable acquisition economics before choosing budgets.
- Gross profit per first purchase/deal = revenue - variable fulfillment/product costs.
- Break-even CAC depends on gross profit and acceptable first-sale contribution.
- Target CAC must leave room for overhead/profit unless intentionally acquiring near break-even based on reliable LTV.
- For lead gen: Target CPL = target CAC x realistic lead-to-customer close rate.
Example: target CAC $300 and 20% lead-to-customer close rate implies a target CPL near $60. This is planning math, not a universal benchmark.

## Delivery system
### Phase 1 — Audit
Business economics -> customer -> offer -> competitor/market -> historical ads -> creative -> landing page -> tracking -> CRM/sales -> compliance.

### Phase 2 — Measurement
Connect/verify Meta assets, Pixel, appropriate standard/custom events, Conversions API where appropriate, domain/event setup as required, UTMs, analytics/CRM, lead source and revenue tracking. Test the complete click-to-conversion journey.

### Phase 3 — Offer and funnel
One audience/problem, one clear offer, one dominant CTA. Ad promise must match landing-page promise. Remove unnecessary friction. Add truthful proof, objection handling, FAQ, trust and clear next step.

### Phase 4 — Creative research
Build a swipe/intelligence file from proven ads and case studies. Do not copy another brand's protected creative. Extract patterns: hook type, awareness stage, angle, format, demonstration, proof, offer, CTA, pacing, visual device, objection, landing-page match.

### Phase 5 — Creative production
Launch materially different concepts rather than cosmetic variations. Starting concept bank:
1. Problem -> solution
2. Desired outcome
3. Product/service demonstration
4. Founder/expert explanation
5. Customer story/testimonial
6. Case-study/data proof
7. Objection handling
8. Comparison/differentiation
9. Education/how-it-works
10. Offer/value stack
11. FAQ
12. Native/UGC-style narrative
13. Branded/direct-response creative
14. Social-proof montage
15. Before/after only when truthful, permitted and policy-compliant

Video skeleton: Pattern interrupt/hook -> problem/desire -> mechanism/value -> proof -> offer -> CTA.

## Creative operating principle
Winning ads are outliers. Motion's 2026 dataset analyzed 578,750 creatives, 6,015 advertiser accounts and $1.29B in Meta spend; roughly 5% met its winner definition. Therefore the system must continuously produce/test creative rather than expect every ad to win. More creative volume correlated with more winners even within similar spend tiers. Treat a winner as a platform for iterations, not a reason to stop testing.

## Campaign architecture
Keep initial structures simple enough to concentrate learning.
- Choose objective closest to the revenue-producing action.
- Service lead gen: usually Leads when lead/application is measurable.
- Ecommerce: usually Sales/Purchase when purchase tracking is reliable.
- Use broad/Advantage+ delivery as a serious baseline when appropriate; test narrower audiences when there is a business reason.
- Advantage+ placements are a sensible default test unless creative/compliance/business requirements justify restrictions.
- Avoid fragmenting small budgets across excessive campaigns/ad sets.
- Retarget when warm audience size justifies it; do not build elaborate retargeting architecture around tiny pools.

## Testing system
Every test needs: hypothesis, variable, primary KPI, guardrail metrics, minimum decision window/data requirement, result, learning, next action.

Priority order for many accounts:
1. Offer/message-market fit
2. Creative concept/angle/hook
3. Landing page/message match
4. Audience/delivery strategy
5. Bid/budget architecture once conversion volume supports it

When a clean causal answer matters, isolate the major variable. Meta also offers creative A/B testing in eligible setups; use controlled tests when ordinary optimized delivery would starve alternatives.

## Performance scorecard
Business outcomes: revenue, gross profit, customers, CAC, LTV, MER/blended efficiency when relevant, qualified leads, appointments, show rate, close rate.
Media diagnostics: spend, impressions, reach, frequency, CPM, link CTR, CPC, landing-page views, CVR, CPL, CPA, ROAS.

Never call an ad successful solely because CTR or CPL is attractive. Cheap unqualified leads can destroy economics.

## Diagnostic decision tree
High CPM -> inspect market/competition, audience constraints, relevance, placement mix, seasonality.
Low CTR -> inspect first-frame/hook, angle, creative clarity, offer and message-market match.
Good CTR + weak landing-page CVR -> inspect speed, congruence, trust, UX, proof, offer and friction.
Good leads + poor booking -> inspect speed-to-lead, nurture and booking friction.
Booked calls + poor show rate -> reminders, qualification and scheduling process.
Qualified calls + poor close rate -> sales process, price/value, objection handling, offer.
Good CAC + constrained volume -> increase creative supply, broaden delivery where sensible, test budget expansion and ensure fulfillment capacity.

## Scaling
Scale only when economics are repeatable enough to justify it.
Vertical: increase budget while watching marginal CAC/ROAS and operational capacity.
Horizontal: new creative concepts/iterations, offers, geographies, audiences, funnels and placements when justified.
Do not declare victory from one strong day.

## Creative iteration system
For each winner, preserve the core reason it likely works and create controlled variations:
- new opening hook
- new first frame
- new creator/persona
- shorter/longer cut
- different proof
- different objection
- alternate headline/CTA
- branded vs native presentation
- static/carousel/video translation
- awareness-stage adaptation

Case evidence: a Laura Geller creative analyzed by Dara Denney/Motion evolved from UGC insight into a '5 Minute Makeup Routine' concept and a grid/spreadsheet-style execution reportedly ran for 200+ days. Sans reported branded content beating UGC in its account ($18 vs $29 CPA), reinforcing that format dogma should yield to account data.

## Lead-response SOP
New lead -> immediate CRM capture -> instant confirmation -> fast human outreach -> structured follow-up -> qualification -> booking -> reminders -> show/no-show -> outcome -> revenue -> source attribution. Track response time and lead disposition. Feed qualified/downstream outcomes back to ad systems where technically appropriate.

## Non-negotiables
Truthful claims. Permission for customer proof. No fake urgency/scarcity. No fabricated testimonials. No hidden ad-spend markup. No guaranteeing ROAS. Protect client data. Document changes. Maintain account ownership/access clarity. Follow Meta policies and applicable advertising law.
