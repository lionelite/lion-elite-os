# Winning Ads Research Intelligence

## Purpose
Study ads/products/funnels that have already demonstrated meaningful performance, identify recurring direct-response patterns, and adapt those principles. Do not reinvent the wheel and do not blindly clone competitors.

## 10-source rule
Before turning a broad strategic claim into an agency-wide recommendation, consult at least 10 independent publishers/operators when enough relevant evidence exists. One publisher = one source regardless of number of articles. For tactical execution, repeatedly observed practitioner patterns can be used as starting plays, then validated in account data.

## Source priority
1. Named brand case study with actual outcome data
2. Large multi-account dataset
3. Experienced operator showing account/creative evidence
4. Platform documentation for mechanics/policy
5. Generic opinion/article

## Initial evidence cards
### Motion — cross-account creative benchmarks (2026)
Dataset: $1.29B Meta spend; 578,750 creatives; 6,015 advertiser accounts. Roughly 5% met Motion's winner definition (>=10x account median spend and >=$500). More weekly creative volume correlated with more winners even controlling for spend tier.
Principle: creative production/testing is a throughput game; expect many non-winners and build a repeatable pipeline.
Caveat: winner classification is based on spend allocation, not directly ROAS/revenue.

### Obvi — health/wellness
Source: Motion customer story. Obvi is described as an 8-figure collagen brand; reported 4x more winning creative and $500K+ monthly ad spend optimized.
Principle: centralize creative analytics and use performance data to inform the next production cycle instead of relying on taste alone.
Caveat: vendor case study; no randomized causal proof that the software itself produced the outcome.

### Sans — home wellness
Source: Motion case-study talk. Reported branded content CPA $18 vs UGC $29 for its account/price point.
Principle: do not assume UGC always wins. Test native/UGC and polished/branded executions against the actual offer and customer.
Caveat: single advertiser/account result.

### Laura Geller — beauty
Source: Dara Denney presentation hosted by Motion. Winning messaging emerged from UGC research; '5 Minute Makeup Routine' and grid/spreadsheet-like creative were highlighted, with one execution reportedly running 200+ days.
Principle: mine customer/UGC language for angles; iterate proven concepts into new formats; broad awareness-stage messaging may scale.
Caveat: practitioner case presentation, not independent controlled research.

### DSW — retail/omnichannel
Source: Tinuiti. Reported +23% omni purchases, +16% revenue lift and -29% incremental CPA using offline conversion data and a conversion-lift test with holdout.
Principle: optimize/measure the actual business outcome, including offline sales where relevant, instead of trusting online-only platform attribution.
Caveat: agency case study; implementation may not generalize to smaller advertisers.

### WordStream/LocaliQ — 2025 benchmark context
Data: 1,000+ Meta campaigns. Lead CPL varied dramatically by vertical; Health & Fitness median CPL reported $52.98 and Beauty & Personal Care $51.42 in the sample.
Principle: benchmark by vertical but set targets from client economics; a universal 'good CPL' is meaningless.
Caveat: sample composition and campaign types differ from any specific client.

## What to steal ethically
Steal the principle: hook structure, awareness strategy, proof architecture, offer logic, pacing, testing method, funnel congruence.
Do not steal: another brand's footage, copy verbatim, trademarks, testimonials, proprietary assets or fabricated results.

## Decision rule
Anecdotal repetition earns a tactic a test. Cross-account data increases confidence. Platform documentation confirms feasibility/mechanics. Our own verified economics determine whether the tactic stays.
