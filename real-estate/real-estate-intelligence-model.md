# Lion Elite Real Estate Intelligence Model

## Purpose

Build a cautious, evidence-driven acquisition intelligence system for Lion Elite's real-estate investments. The system must help business partners compare properties, verify claims, identify hidden risks, coordinate third-party professionals, and make documented investment decisions.

The model is a decision-support system. It must never treat listing language, seller statements, estimated rents, automated valuations, or unverified third-party data as confirmed facts.

## Core Operating Rules

1. Separate confirmed facts, seller claims, listing claims, estimates, and assumptions.
2. Attach a source and retrieval date to every material fact.
3. Never use projected rent when an executed lease and rent ledger are available.
4. Never recommend removing inspection, financing, appraisal, title, insurance, or document-review contingencies without written approval from qualified counsel and the investment committee.
5. Flag missing information instead of filling gaps with guesses.
6. Require independent verification of seller disclosures and property condition.
7. Preserve an audit trail of documents, questions, answers, inspections, revisions, and approvals.
8. Require human approval before making offers, hiring vendors, committing funds, or sending representations to lenders or sellers.

## Seller-Motivation Analysis

The model must document, but not assume, why the owner is selling.

### Required questions for the listing agent or seller

- Why is the owner selling now?
- How long has the owner held the property?
- Is the sale related to retirement, estate planning, relocation, partnership dissolution, refinancing difficulty, insurance costs, taxes, maintenance, tenant problems, or a 1031 exchange?
- Has the property previously been under contract?
- If so, why did each contract terminate?
- Were prior inspection, appraisal, title, insurance, financing, or tenant issues identified?
- Have offers been rejected, and on what general terms?
- Are there deadlines affecting the seller?
- Are there any pending insurance claims, lawsuits, code cases, evictions, or disputes?
- Has the owner received notices regarding recertification, unsafe structures, zoning, flooding, or municipal compliance?

### Motivation confidence levels

- **Confirmed:** Supported by seller documentation, attorney communication, public records, or multiple consistent sources.
- **Reported:** Stated by the seller or listing agent but not independently verified.
- **Inferred:** Suggested by listing history, ownership records, liens, vacancy, price reductions, or other circumstantial evidence.
- **Unknown:** No reliable explanation obtained.

The final report must never present an inferred motive as fact.

## Financial Underwriting

### Required source documents

- Current certified rent roll
- All leases and amendments
- Tenant ledger for at least 12 months, preferably 24
- Security-deposit ledger
- Trailing 12-month operating statement
- Prior two years of operating statements when available
- Property-tax bills
- Insurance declarations and invoices
- Utility bills
- Repair and maintenance invoices
- Capital-expenditure records
- Existing property-management statements
- Service contracts
- Current mortgage or payoff information when relevant to transaction structure

### Core calculations

- Gross potential rent
- Effective gross income
- Vacancy and credit loss
- Other income
- Operating expenses
- Net operating income
- Capitalization rate
- Debt-service coverage ratio
- Monthly and annual debt service
- Cash flow before tax
- Cash-on-cash return
- Break-even occupancy
- Required operating reserves
- Immediate repair budget
- Five-year capital-expenditure reserve
- Stabilized value based on independently supported assumptions
- Sensitivity analysis for rents, vacancy, insurance, taxes, repairs, interest rate, and management fees

### Underwriting standards

The model must show at least three cases:

1. **Current operations:** Existing leases, current expenses, and no speculative rent increase.
2. **Conservative stabilized case:** Supported market rents, realistic vacancy, management, reserves, and verified repairs.
3. **Stress case:** Lower rents or collections, higher vacancy, higher insurance, higher repairs, and unfavorable financing.

## Physical Due Diligence

### Primary independent inspector

Hire a Florida-licensed inspector experienced with small multifamily or commercial residential properties. The general inspector coordinates the initial whole-property assessment but does not replace licensed specialists.

### Required or condition-triggered specialists

- Licensed roofing contractor
- Licensed electrician
- Licensed plumber
- HVAC contractor
- Sewer-line camera specialist
- Wood-destroying organism or termite inspector
- Mold assessor when moisture evidence or history exists
- Structural engineer when cracking, settlement, framing, balcony, foundation, or load-bearing concerns exist
- Environmental specialist when tanks, contamination, asbestos, lead paint, or hazardous materials may be present
- Pool contractor when applicable
- Elevator contractor when applicable
- General contractor for repair pricing
- Accessibility or code consultant when applicable

### Inspection deliverables

Each inspection record must include:

- Vendor name, license, insurance, and conflicts disclosure
- Inspection scope
- Date completed
- Photographs and video
- Observed defect
- Severity
- Safety impact
- Code or insurance impact
- Remaining useful life
- Repair recommendation
- Estimated cost range
- Whether invasive testing is recommended
- Whether reinspection is required

## Risk Scoring

Each property receives a 0-100 score supported by evidence.

### Suggested weights

- Financial performance and debt coverage: 25
- Physical condition and capital needs: 20
- Legal unit status, title, zoning, and permits: 15
- Insurance, flood, wind, and climate exposure: 15
- Tenant and lease quality: 10
- Location and rental-market durability: 10
- Seller transparency and data completeness: 5

### Automatic high-risk flags

- Unit count cannot be legally verified
- Seller refuses leases, rent records, expenses, or inspection access
- Open unsafe-structure issue
- Uninsurable roof, electrical, plumbing, or building condition
- Material unpermitted work
- Negative cash flow in the conservative case
- DSCR below lender requirement
- Major unresolved title or zoning issue
- Active structural concern without engineering clearance
- Existing tenants or deposits not properly documented
- Material mismatch between listing claims and verified records

## Decision Statuses

- **Researching:** Initial data collection underway.
- **Awaiting seller documents:** Material seller information missing.
- **Preliminary pass:** Worth further expense, not yet approved.
- **Due diligence:** Inspections, title, financing, insurance, and management review active.
- **Renegotiate:** Deal only works with price, credit, repairs, or terms adjusted.
- **Approve with conditions:** Investment committee approval subject to listed requirements.
- **Approved:** All required conditions documented and accepted.
- **Reject:** Risk, return, documentation, or financing does not meet standards.
- **Monitor:** Not acceptable now but worth tracking for price or condition changes.

4. Ownership and listing history
5. Unit and tenant summary
6. Current, conservative, and stress underwriting

17. Final decision and approval signatures

## Initial Five-Property Workflow

For each of the first five Miami multifamily candidates:

1. Confirm the listing is active and the address is correct.
2. Obtain the listing history and public ownership record.
3. Send the standardized seller-question request.
4. Request all financial and tenant documents.
5. Verify legal unit count, zoning, permits, and code history.
6. Request preliminary insurance indications.
7. Obtain property-management rent opinions from three firms.
8. Build current, conservative, and stress underwriting.
9. Rank the five properties before paying for full inspections.
10. Inspect only the strongest candidates unless contract timing requires otherwise.
11. Update rankings after inspections, insurance, title, appraisal, and lender review.
12. Present a documented investment committee recommendation.

## Data Ethics and Compliance

Use lawful, authorized, and appropriately licensed data sources. Respect website terms, privacy requirements, fair-housing laws, consumer-reporting restrictions, tenant confidentiality, lender rules, and professional licensing boundaries. Do not scrape restricted personal data or make protected-class inferences. Tenant screening and lending decisions must remain with authorized professionals using compliant processes.
