# Client agreement — required terms

> **Not legal advice and not an agreement.** The terms an attorney needs to draft
> or review the master agreement and each statement of work. The generated
> proposal is a commercial document, not a contract.

## Structure

A master services agreement signed once, plus a statement of work per
engagement. The SOW carries scope and price; the MSA carries everything below.
Do not merge them — you will renegotiate the whole relationship every project.

## Terms the model depends on

**Parties.** The client contracts with **our company**. Contractors are not
parties, are not named, and the client acquires no relationship with them.

**Fixed price.** The SOW price is fixed for the scope in the SOW. Changes go
through a written change order with its own price. No hourly billing.

**Payment.** 50–70% on signature, balance on final acceptance. Name the
consequence of non-payment: work stops, and nothing is deployed or handed over.

**Acceptance.** Each milestone is accepted against the acceptance criteria in the
SOW — written, objective, agreed before work starts. Give the client a defined
review window, and deem it accepted if they do not respond within it. Without
that, one distracted client stalls the project indefinitely.

**IP.** The client gets ownership of, or a perpetual licence to, the deliverables
on **final payment** — not before. We retain our pre-existing tools, libraries,
and general methods, and the right to reuse generic, non-client-specific
components. Say so explicitly: it is what makes the next build cheaper.

**No performance guarantee.** We warrant the system meets the acceptance
criteria. We do not guarantee a revenue figure. The proposal's value model is an
estimate built on the client's own reported inputs, and should say so in the
agreement too.

**Client dependencies.** Access, approvals, and a named decision-maker, with
defined response times. Delay caused by the client moves the schedule and does
not reduce the price.

**Data protection.** Who holds what, where, and under whose accounts. A BAA where
PHI is in scope. What happens to data on termination.

**Third-party costs.** Software, messaging, telephony and model-usage costs are
the client's, on the client's accounts. Never front these.

**Retainer.** Separate term or separate agreement: monthly or quarterly, what it
covers, what it does not, notice period to cancel. Define a response-time
commitment or it becomes unbounded.

**Liability.** Cap it, conventionally at fees paid. Exclude consequential and
indirect loss. This is the clause that decides whether a bad engagement is a
refund or a business-ending event — have an attorney write it.

**Termination.** Either side, with notice. On termination the client pays for
accepted milestones; we hand over what was accepted and paid for.

**Confidentiality.** Mutual.

## Watch for

- A client-supplied MSA with an **hourly rate** or **unlimited revisions** clause.
- **Uncapped liability** or an indemnity with no cap.
- **Acceptance with no deadline** — the stall risk above.
- **IP passing on signature** rather than on final payment.
- A **revenue guarantee** or performance-penalty clause. Decline it; the engine
  disqualifies prospects who insist.
